# Results

## Key Electrochemical Findings

The SiGC composite anodes were evaluated using electrochemical characterization techniques including cyclic voltammetry and galvanostatic charge-discharge cycling.

### Specific Capacity

The reported specific capacities for the investigated SiGC electrodes were approximately:

| Si Treatment | Reported Specific Capacity |
|---|---:|
| Unsieved Si | ~160 mAh/g |
| 100 µm Sieved Si | ~158.31 mAh/g |

The manuscript reports slightly higher initial capacity for the unsieved Si-containing composite.

### Cycling Performance

After 100 cycles:

| Si Treatment | Capacity Retention |
|---|---:|
| Unsieved Si | 76.5% |
| 100 µm Sieved Si | 85% |

The sieved Si composite demonstrated improved cycling retention despite having a slightly lower initial specific capacity.

## Cyclic Voltammetry

For the representative B2 cell, cyclic voltammetry was performed between 0.005 and 1.5 V at a scan rate of 0.1 mV/s.

The first cycle showed behavior associated with initial irreversible processes, while subsequent cycles exhibited comparatively consistent behavior.

## Structural and Microstructural Characterization

X-ray diffraction identified the characteristic phases associated with graphite and crystalline silicon.

TEM and elemental mapping were used to investigate the distribution of silicon and carbon within the composite. The manuscript reports carbon encapsulation/coating around nano-Si and its interaction with the graphite matrix.

## Key Takeaway

Sieve treatment of the silicon powder produced a SiGC composite with:

- Comparable specific capacity to the unsieved composite
- Improved capacity retention over 100 cycles
- More consistent long-term electrochemical performance

The results indicate that silicon particle-size control can influence the electrochemical stability of SiGC composite anodes.
